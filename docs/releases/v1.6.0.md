**1.6.0 — external catalog cross-check, agreement score, 62 new locks**

The chest database is now cross-checked against three community lock catalogs. A transcription error, such as a missing dependency, a flipped sign or positions typed in reverse plate order, can still describe a solvable lock. Solvability alone can't reveal it; you may only notice in game, when a plate hits the wall and the pick breaks. Comparing independent records of the same lock can flag such an entry before that happens.

### New
- **62 new locks**: Bandit Camp, the mercenaries' and rogues' chests in the New Camp, the Old Camp armory, the mages' rooms, the guards' chests and more. Names are in all four languages, and every entry is solvable as recorded.
- **Agreement with other sources.** Each entry is scored by how many other catalogs record it the same way:
  - Search results and chest hints list the better-agreeing entry first.
  - Entries that other sources dispute, outweighing the ones that agree, get a warning mark, and the solve stage reminds you to check the starting positions and dependencies against the game.
  - When the database holds a better-agreeing entry for the same lock, the notice offers to open it.
- **Search by asset id** (e.g. `OC_Chest_Guard242_Lock`, presumably the game's own id) where a catalog provides one.

### Removed
- Three entries whose dependencies contradict records of the same locks that were presumably made in game. Replayed on those records, their solution would hit a wall on the 2nd or 10th press: one of the two entries for Scatty's ring chest and two of the entries for the chest above the Arena. The entries for these chests that agree with other sources remain.

### For contributors
- `npm run sync:catalogs` compares the database with the community catalogs and writes a lock-by-lock comparison matrix. The full update workflow is in the `update-chest-db` skill.
- `npm start` serves the app at http://localhost:3000.
- A new end-to-end test solves real chests and checks every step card and 3D-scene position against an independent solver.
